Calian, 2026

AgileX Scout Mini

A small four wheel drive base with independent suspension, fitted out to know where it is and what's around it. The parts on top are what turn it from a remote control car into a robot that can find its own way.

Two GNSS antennas

One at the front and one at the back, on the robot's center line. Comparing the signal at each one tells the robot which way it is facing, even when it's standing still.

Depth camera

An Intel RealSense D455, centered under the front antenna, sees in 3D, so the robot knows how far away everything in front of it is.

IMU and Raspberry Pi

A BNO085 inertial sensor in the back right corner tracks turns and tilt between satellite fixes. The Raspberry Pi next to it runs ROS 2 and ties every sensor together.

Queen's Knights Robotics, 2024 to now

A RoboMaster style robot

Our team builds robots for ARCC 2026, a competition where robots fire small projectiles at each other's armor plates while an onboard referee system keeps score. I'm one of the electrical engineers.

Mecanum wheels

Rollers set at 45 degrees let the robot slide sideways and spin in place, so it can dodge while the turret keeps aiming.

Armor modules

Each plate senses hits and reports them to the referee system, which takes health away. Lose it all and you're out.

Gimbal and launcher

Two motors aim the launcher: one turns the whole turret through a pair of gears, the other tilts the barrel. Two spinning wheels fire the 42 mm balls, and a speed monitor at the muzzle checks every shot stays under the speed limit.

Power, which is my part

I design high energy capacitor banks and the protection for our battery management system, and I pick the MOSFETs and IGBTs that can take the motor loads when the robot is pushed hard.

Queen's Aerospace Design Team, 2025 to 2026

A banner towing bush plane

The team's plane for AIAA Design Build Fly 2026: three fast laps empty, a charter flight full of passengers, then a banner unrolled in flight and dropped. It's a mid wing design with a 1.5 m span, laid up by hand in carbon fiber.

I was on the research team that season, working on a VTOL prototype: propulsion, avionics, and tuning PX4 until it could hover steadily and fly on its own.

Two motors on the leading edge

Two 580 KV brushless motors swing 11 by 6 propellers, fed by one 6S battery in the belly.

The passengers are rubber ducks

The charter mission is scored on how many passengers and how much cargo you carry. This one seats nine ducks and three pucks.

The banner

It rides outside the plane on takeoff. Mid flight it unrolls on lines routed through the tail, and when a pin is pulled it lets go and drops to the ground.

FIRST Global Challenge

A FIRST Global robot

Every team builds from the same kit of REV parts: aluminum extrusion, motors, omni wheels and control hubs. With everyone on the same parts, the difference comes down to design and code. I was my team's programmer in Geneva in 2022, and I've mentored teams every year since.

Intake

A roller of orange flaps spins at the front and pulls the red balls in off the floor.

The hubs

One REV hub on each side wall. Between them they wire up every motor and servo, and the robot's code runs on them.

Two arms

They reach back over the big ball, with belts driving small omni wheels at their tips.

Course project, Queen's

The Quackston line follower

A small robot that drives itself around the Town of Quackston, a model city with roads, intersections and AprilTag signs. Ours carried a duck and its treasure under a canopy of vines.

Grayscale sensor

Three sensors under the nose read the line. When the middle one drifts off, the robot steers back.

Ultrasonic sensor

Pings ahead and waits for the echo, so the robot stops before it runs into anything.

Raspberry Pi and Robot HAT

The Pi does the thinking and the HAT drives the motors and servo. The three LEDs on the mini breadboard show what it's doing.

The passenger

Every robot in Quackston has a duck to deliver. Ours rode on its own chest of gold.

Canadian Space Agency project, 2024

Lunar rover

A small rover designed to cross the lunar surface, built over twelve weeks. I led the project and worked on the electrical integration, the SOLIDWORKS models and the traction.

Ultrasonic eyes

Two transducers on the nose, one to ping and one to listen, so it can see rocks before it hits them.

Paddle treads

Loose regolith swallows smooth tires. The paddles dig in and push off the ground instead.

Printed shell

The whole body is 3D printed, with a lid that comes off so the electronics stay easy to reach.

ELEC 374, Queen's, with two classmates

Mini SRC processor

Three of us built a 32 bit processor in Verilog, tested every instruction in simulation, then ran it on a DE0 CV FPGA board. My part was the ALU: a carry lookahead adder for add and subtract, plus the logic, shift, rotate and negate operations. I also built the load and store paths and the CON FF, the small circuit that decides whether a branch is taken.

In our final version instructions come from their own memory, so a fetch takes one clock instead of three. On the board it reads the switches, writes to the seven segment displays and handles interrupts from a button.

    Automated Cultivation Environment

    Ashwagandha is a medicinal plant with real cultural and health value, and it's fussy about how it grows. Our planter holds the conditions it likes with sensors and automatic climate control. Spools on the threaded rod at the front pull the plastic cover shut over the soil, or let it gather at the back when the plants want sun, and a web interface lets you watch it all and adjust it.
